# Market prices on a long list (0.710)

User request, 2026-11-07: faster on a large demand, without troubling the site.

## What was slow

The capture showed 64 of 358 after a while. A list of 32 sent one request, and the next list came before the 3-second pause was over, so `FillCacheAsync` gave up and every item went on its own, 3 seconds each. An item with no price also cost its own request. And the memory kept 128 quotes: the first ones of a long list were gone before its end.

## What it does now

On the two long lists (What to do with it?, What can I craft?) only:

- One request at a time, 100 items, the limit measured in `docs/evidence/0.608-universalis-batch.md`.
- One second between them, waiting its turn rather than falling back on single requests: about 100 prices a second at best, against 3 seconds an item.
- The answer already covers items with no price (`failedItems`), so they cost nothing more.
- A 429 or 503 stops the list and holds further lists for 30 seconds, or longer if `Retry-After` says so. The page shows the failure with Try again.
- The Universalis switch and the box on each page still apply. Prices already fresh in the cache are not asked again.

Other pages keep the 3-second pause, and hover requests wait while a list runs.

## Not established

Universalis's rate limit: the documentation page read on 2026-11-07 gave no text, a search found nothing, and its answers carry no rate header. One request a second, one at a time, is a choice of the agent's on the cautious side, not a figure from the site.
